Clusters sit on stressed ground more than bare land area predicts

{{ extreme_compact|length }} of {{ dc_sites_compact|length }} confirmed Compact-state sites ({{ '%.0f'|format(100.0 * extreme_compact|length / dc_sites_compact|length) }}%) sit in a watershed reading extremely high -- Chandler/Mesa AZ, all of Denver CO, Los Lunas NM, all of Cheyenne WY -- well above the {{ '%.1f'|format(compact_share) }}% area-weighted regional baseline. Not universal: Goodyear AZ, Silicon Valley CA, Las Vegas NV, and both Utah sites read low. The same in-state split found in WA/OR recurs here too: Chandler/Mesa reads extremely high while Goodyear, same Phoenix metro, reads low; the Tahoe-Reno cluster reads high while Las Vegas, same state, reads low.

Independent corroboration

Oregon designated the Walla Walla Basin a "Serious Water Management Problem Area" in 2017 after basalt-aquifer declines of 1-4 ft/year; Washington Ecology's parallel "Walla Walla 2050" initiative covers the same bi-state basin. Aqueduct is a global model with no knowledge of either designation and lands on the same footprint anyway.

What this doesn't measure

This is local withdrawal-to-supply ratio, not Colorado River allocation risk — Nevada and Utah both read low despite heavy Colorado River exposure, because Las Vegas draws ~90% of its water from Lake Mead while sitting in a Great Basin internal-drainage watershed. It's a long-run average, not current drought conditions. And it says nothing about water quality, which this page does not attempt to show.
