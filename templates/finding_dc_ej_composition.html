The data-center poverty gap is Simpson's Paradox

ZIP codes hosting a confirmed data center score poorer on average than ones that don't. Split the same ZIP codes by race and the gap all but disappears inside each group. Pool the two groups back together and it reappears -- because hosting ZIP codes simply have a different racial mix, and minority poverty runs structurally higher everywhere in the country, independent of data centers.

Why this is Simpson's Paradox

Simpson's Paradox is what happens when pooling two groups at a different mix manufactures a trend that isn't sitting inside either group alone. The most famous real case: UC Berkeley's 1973 graduate admissions looked biased against women in aggregate (44.2% of men admitted vs. 34.6% of women), but broken out by department, the overall pattern reversed -- only 4 of 85 departments were significantly biased against women, 6 against men, and the majority showed no real gap either way (Bickel, Hammel & O'Connell, 1975). Women were applying more to the departments that rejected nearly everyone; blend a low-admit department and a high-admit department together and the blend just reflects who applied where.

Same shape here. Hosting ZIP codes have a meaningfully different racial mix than non-hosting ones ({{ '%.1f'|format(minority_share_hosting * 100) }}% vs. {{ '%.1f'|format(minority_share_nonhosting * 100) }}% minority population share), and minority poverty runs structurally higher than white poverty everywhere in the country, with or without a data center anywhere nearby. Blend two populations that individually show close to parity, at a different ratio, and the blend inherits whichever group's rate the mix leans toward. That's arithmetic, not evidence that a data center changed anyone's life.

What this doesn't show

This is a 2022 cross-section, not a before/after test -- no site in this dataset has a known opening date, so selection (data centers landing where the demographic mix already looked like this) and impact (a data center changing the mix after arriving) can't be distinguished here. It also covers only the demographic half of Merascope's EJ score; the pollution and hazard half is a separate, EPA-sourced measurement not included on this page.
