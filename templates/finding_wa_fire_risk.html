Microsoft's own East Wenatchee campus sits inside a documented fire perimeter

EAT06/EAT09 (under construction) sits directly inside the 2005 Badger Road Fire's MTBS-mapped perimeter (3,993 acres) -- WHP's own hazard model reads that ground as "low," unaware a real fire already burned there. Every other confirmed WA site reads WHP "non-burnable" or "low" on its own parcel and sits outside every MTBS-mapped perimeter, expected since data centers are mostly built on developed/agricultural land. WHP's fuel model reads that same ag/CRP land as non-burnable even where real fire has reached close by -- so distance to an actual historical burn (not model-dependent) is the sharper number: Malaga sits just 0.9 km from a real fire (1.4 km from WHP high/very-high hazard land), the rest of the Quincy cluster 3.8-4.3 km out, Wallula Gap 7.9 km out, while every Seattle-metro site sits 47-50 km from any historical burn, agreeing with the model there. Wildfire threatens built infrastructure through nearby wildland fuel and ember cast, not just the parcel footprint.

What this doesn't measure

WHP's land-cover model reads developed and agricultural land as "non-burnable" by definition — including Conservation Reserve Program grassland, which is exactly what carried the 2020 Pearl Hill and Cold Springs fires (187,000-218,000 acres each) through the same shrub-steppe interior this data reads as low-hazard, and it has no memory of fires that already happened, as EAT06/EAT09 shows directly. That's a documented limitation of forest/rangeland-oriented hazard models -- patched above by the real-burn-distance column, not papered over. Separately, MTBS's identification of individual named fires is not exhaustive by name — large multi-fire complexes are sometimes mapped under original component-fire names rather than a merged complex name.
